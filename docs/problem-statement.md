Problem Statement - Clinic Queue Management System

Course: CEF345 - Groupe 1
Project: clinic-queue
Team Role: Analysis - Nyetalem, Ticha, Ajonina
Client: Small Private Clinic in Buea (General Consultation)
Date: 10/11/2026

 1. Who is the Client?

The client is a small private clinic in Buea that receives 40-80 patients per day for general consultation, lab tests, and pharmacy.

Staff involved:
- 1 Receptionist / Cashier
- 2-3 Nurses
- 1-2 Doctors (General Practitioner)
- 1 Lab technician
- Patients (adults, children, elderly)

Currently, the clinic uses no digital queue system. Everything is done manually.

 2. Current Situation - How It Works Today

1. A patient arrives and writes their name in a paper notebook at reception.
2. The receptionist calls names verbally from memory or from the notebook.
3. Patients sit in a crowded waiting room. They do not know when their turn will come.
4. After consultation, the patient is sent to the lab or pharmacy, where they join another manual queue.
5. At the end of the day, the receptionist counts the names in the notebook to know how many patients came.

 3. Problems Identified (From Observation / Interview)

 Problem 1: Long and Unpredictable Waiting Time
Patients wait 2-4 hours without knowing their position in the queue. 
They cannot leave to buy water or go to the pharmacy because they are afraid of missing their turn when their name is called. Elderly and sick patients suffer the most.

 Problem 2: Queue Jumping and Conflicts
Because the queue is on paper and calling is verbal, patients argue about who came first. 
Friends / relatives of staff are sometimes called earlier. This creates conflict at reception and loss of trust in the clinic.

 Problem 3: No Record and No Follow-up
The paper notebook is often lost, torn, or has unreadable handwriting. 
The clinic cannot know: How many patients waited? How long did they wait? Which doctor saw the most patients? 
There is no data to improve the service.

 Problem 4: Patients Leave Without Being Seen
When the waiting room is full, some patients leave without telling reception. 
Their names are still called later, wasting time. The doctor waits for a patient who has already left.

 Problem 5: No Communication Between Reception, Doctor and Lab
After consultation, the patient must physically carry a paper to the lab. 
The lab does not know a patient is coming. If the paper is lost, the patient must return to the doctor to get a new one.

 4. Who is Affected?

- Patients: Waste time, stress, conflicts, leave without care.
- Receptionist: Overwhelmed, blamed for queue problems, manual work.
- Doctors / Nurses: Lose time calling absent patients, no clear order of patients.
- Clinic Owner: Loses patients who go to other clinics, no statistics to manage the clinic.

 5. What Success Looks Like (Without Features)

A good solution should help the clinic to:
- Reduce confusion about who is next
- Make waiting time fair and visible to patients
- Keep a reliable record of patients seen per day
- Reduce conflicts at reception

 6. Out of Scope

This problem statement does NOT cover:
- Online payment / Mobile Money
- Online doctor consultation (telemedicine)
- Pharmacy stock management
- Full electronic medical records (EMR)

We focus only on the queue and patient flow inside the clinic.

 7. Sources

- Observation: Visit to a private clinic in Buea
- Informal interview: Receptionist and 2 patients waiting in queue
